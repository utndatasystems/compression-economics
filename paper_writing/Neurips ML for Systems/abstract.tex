Large language models (LLMs) can be powerful lossless compressors:
their next-token probabilities coupled with entropy coding can reduce data
below its original size. Yet existing evaluations largely single out
predictive performance of the LLM, leaving an important systems
question understudied: when does compression survive severe model
mismatch? We show that the answer lies in treating LLM-based
compression as a two-layer system of (1) tokenization as a stable,
queryable representation and (2) predictive entropy coding as an
optional cold-storage layer, guarded by a block-level selector that
stores the smaller of Qwen+AC and Brotli. On text8, Qwen2.5-0.5B
compresses to $17.6\%$ of raw size,
outperforming tokenization-only as compression ($41.3\%$) and the conventional compression algorithsm Brotli ($29.6\%$). Selecting
the least-probable valid next token requires a prediction rate $5.68\times$ that of
text8, measured in bits/token, yet long tokens buffer these
errors and keep the complete data stream at $36.2\%$. Restricting
candidates to one-byte tokens only removes this buffer and expands the
complete stream to $122.8\%$, while conventional codecs remain
compressive. We therefore introduce an objective that maximizes
decoded-byte-normalized surprisal, attacking the buffer directly and producing
an arithmetic payload of $122.4\%$ and a complete stream of $144.3\%$ of raw size.
